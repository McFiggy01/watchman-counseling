What Anxiety Can Look Like

◆

Racing or Intrusive Thoughts

Your mind won't stop. You replay conversations, anticipate problems, and can't seem to get off the worry loop no matter how hard you try.

◆

Physical Tension & Tightness

A tight chest, shallow breathing, clenched jaw, headaches — anxiety doesn't just live in your head. Your body carries it too.

◆

Avoidance

Staying away from situations, people, or places that trigger worry — even when avoiding them is making your world smaller.

◆

Sleep Problems & Exhaustion

Can't fall asleep. Wake up at 3am. Run on empty all day. Anxiety and sleep are deeply connected, and poor rest makes everything harder.

◆

Constant Worst-Case Thinking

Your brain skips straight to the worst possible outcome. Even when things are fine, something feels like it's about to go wrong.

◆

On Edge When Nothing's Wrong

A low-grade tension that never fully lifts. You can't relax even when you have permission to — and that in itself is exhausting.

The approach

How We Work With Anxiety

Anxiety therapy at Watchman Counseling is grounded in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) — one of the most researched and effective approaches for anxiety disorders. The goal isn't just to feel calmer in session. It's to understand what's driving the anxiety and build the skills to respond to it differently in your actual daily life.

That means identifying the thought patterns that fuel worry — the distortions, the catastrophizing, the assumptions you've been making on autopilot. Then we challenge them. Not by telling you to "just think positive," but by looking at the evidence, testing the beliefs, and building a more grounded way of reading situations.

We also work on the behavioral piece — the avoidance, the safety behaviors, the things anxiety has quietly convinced you to stop doing. Gradually and at your pace, we build tolerance and get your life back.

Hector L. Figueroa, LCSW has worked with anxiety across a range of clinical settings — from outpatient mental health to intensive community-based work. He brings both clinical rigor and genuine human warmth to the work.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does anxiety therapy take? +
It varies by person and severity, but many clients notice meaningful change within 8–12 sessions. CBT for anxiety is a focused, structured approach — not open-ended indefinitely. We'll set clear goals early and check in on progress regularly. Some people work with me for a few months; others continue longer as new challenges come up.
Is online therapy effective for anxiety? +
Yes — research consistently shows that telehealth CBT is as effective as in-person therapy for anxiety. For many people, being able to work from home actually reduces one barrier to care (the commute, the waiting room, the exposure) and makes it easier to build real skills in the environment where they actually live.
What if I've tried therapy before and it didn't help? +
That's worth talking about — and it doesn't mean therapy can't work for you. Sometimes the approach wasn't the right fit, the timing wasn't right, or the therapeutic relationship wasn't there. CBT for anxiety is different from general talk therapy; it's structured, skill-based, and focused on specific outcomes. I'd rather have that conversation directly than have you rule yourself out before we start.
