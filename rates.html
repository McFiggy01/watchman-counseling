Why I Practice Private Pay

This is a deliberate choice — and it benefits you as much as it does me.

Your privacy stays intact

When you use insurance, a mental health diagnosis goes on your permanent health record — visible to insurers, some employers, and certain government programs. Private pay clients are not required to carry a diagnosis. What we discuss stays between us.

You drive the work, not a reviewer

Insurance companies authorize a fixed number of sessions and approve the type of care you receive. With private pay, you and I decide how often we meet, what approach we use, and when you're ready to slow down or stop. No third party reviews your case.

Lower caseload, more attention

Insurance reimbursement rates push therapists to carry 30–40+ clients. I keep my caseload intentionally small so I can show up fully prepared for every session. You are not one of fifty.

Specialized approaches, your way

Insurance plans often limit therapy to specific, protocol-driven methods. Private pay allows us to draw from CBT, trauma-informed care, motivational interviewing, and faith-integrated approaches — whatever actually fits you.

Still want to use your benefits?

If you have a PPO plan, you may be able to submit a superbill — an itemized receipt — directly to your insurer for out-of-network reimbursement. Many clients recover 50–80% of session costs this way. I provide superbills upon request.

To check your benefits, call the member services number on the back of your card and ask: "Do I have out-of-network mental health benefits? What is my deductible and reimbursement rate for outpatient psychotherapy?"

HSA and FSA funds are also fully applicable to therapy. I can provide documentation for reimbursement on request.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the questions most people have before their first session.

Is telehealth as effective as in-person therapy?
Yes. The research consistently shows no meaningful difference in outcomes, client satisfaction, or the therapeutic relationship between telehealth and in-person sessions. Studies published in peer-reviewed journals — including research from JMIR Mental Health and Clinical Psychology: Science and Practice — confirm that telehealth produces equivalent results for anxiety, trauma, depression, and other common presenting concerns. You don't need to be in a physical office for the work to be real.
How often will we meet?
Weekly sessions are the standard starting point and are recommended for most clients, particularly in the early stages of treatment. Consistency is what builds the momentum that makes therapy work. Some clients transition to every-other-week once they have made meaningful progress. We will discuss frequency together and adjust based on your clinical needs and schedule.
What do I need for a telehealth session?
A private space where you can speak freely, a reliable internet connection, and a device with a camera. That's it. Sessions are conducted through a HIPAA-compliant platform. If you have technical difficulties, contact me right away and we will find a solution.
What is your cancellation policy?
I ask for at least 24 hours notice to cancel or reschedule. Late cancellations and no-shows are charged a $75 fee, billed to the card on file. This policy exists because your time slot is held exclusively for you. If a genuine emergency comes up, reach out as soon as possible — real emergencies are handled with grace.
How long will I be in therapy?
There's no universal answer — it depends on what you're working on and the depth of change you're after. Some clients reach their goals in a few months. Others find ongoing monthly or bi-weekly sessions valuable for continued growth. We will check in regularly on your progress and adjust accordingly. This is your process, not a fixed program.
What if I'm not sure I can afford this?
Reach out and let's talk. I won't be able to help everyone, but I can point you in the right direction and have an honest conversation about whether this investment makes sense for where you are right now. A free 15-minute consultation is a good place to start.
