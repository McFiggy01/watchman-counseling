Fees & Insurance

Understanding Your Options

Watchman Counseling is a private-pay practice. That means you pay directly for sessions — but many clients get reimbursed through their insurance. Here's exactly how it works.

Private Pay

You pay directly at the time of session. Rates are discussed during the free 15-minute consult.

Superbills

I provide an itemized superbill after each session so you can submit to your insurance for reimbursement.

Major Cards

I accept all major credit cards, HSA and FSA cards, and can discuss sliding scale on a case-by-case basis.

01

Check Your Out-of-Network Benefits

Even though Watchman Counseling is out-of-network, many PPO insurance plans reimburse a portion of your session costs. Before your first appointment, call the member services number on the back of your insurance card and ask the following questions:

Questions to ask your insurance rep
  • →"Does my plan cover out-of-network mental health services, including outpatient psychotherapy and telehealth?"
  • →"Do I have an out-of-network deductible? If so, how much is remaining before my plan starts reimbursing me?"
  • →"What is the maximum reimbursement for mental health service code 90837 with a psychotherapist?"
  • →"What percentage of the allowed amount does my plan cover for out-of-network mental health visits?"
  • →"Is telehealth covered the same as in-person for out-of-network mental health services?"

Note: These questions apply to individual therapy. If you're seeking a different service type, speak with Hector first to confirm the appropriate CPT codes.

HMO plans typically don't cover out-of-network providers. PPO and POS plans are most likely to have out-of-network benefits. If you're unsure, the insurance rep can tell you your plan type.

02

What Is a Superbill?

A superbill is an itemized receipt your therapist provides after a session. It contains everything your insurance company needs to process a reimbursement claim. I provide superbills upon request — typically monthly or after each session.

What's included on a superbill
✓Patient information — name, date of birth, contact details
✓Provider information — name, license, NPI number, practice address
✓Date(s) of service — the specific appointment dates
✓CPT procedure code — the standardized billing code (e.g., 90837)
✓Diagnosis code (ICD-10) — the clinical diagnosis that justifies the service
✓Place of service code — code 02 for telehealth
✓Fee charged — the amount you paid per session
✓Units — number of sessions billed
03

How to Submit for Reimbursement

Once you have your superbill, the process is straightforward. Most insurance companies let you submit online, by mail, or through their app.

  1. 1
    Request your superbill

    After your session, let me know you need a superbill. I'll send it to you digitally, typically within a few business days.

  2. 2
    Log in to your insurance portal

    Most major insurers (Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, etc.) have an online member portal where you can submit out-of-network claims directly. Look for "Submit a Claim" or "Reimbursement."

  3. 3
    Upload or mail the superbill

    Attach the superbill (PDF or photo) to your claim. If your insurer requires a paper form, you can download it from their website and mail it in with a copy of the superbill.

  4. 4
    Receive your reimbursement

    Processing times vary by insurer — typically 2 to 6 weeks. The check or direct deposit will reflect your plan's out-of-network reimbursement rate after any deductible is met.

Important: Reimbursement is not guaranteed and depends on your specific plan. The amount you receive is determined by your insurer's out-of-network allowed amount and your plan's reimbursement percentage — not by me. I encourage you to confirm your benefits before your first session so there are no surprises.

Common Questions

How much does a session cost? +
Rates are discussed during the free 15-minute consultation. I want to make sure the investment works for you before we begin. I accept all major credit cards, HSA cards, and FSA cards.
Is there a sliding scale? +
A limited number of reduced-fee slots are available on a case-by-case basis. If cost is a concern, mention it during the consultation — I'd rather find a way to make it work than have you go without support.
Will my insurance cover telehealth therapy? +
Many plans do cover telehealth, but coverage varies. When you call your insurer, ask specifically whether out-of-network telehealth mental health services are covered and whether there are any restrictions on video-based therapy sessions.
What is a Good Faith Estimate? +
Under the No Surprises Act, you have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected costs before beginning services. I provide this upon request and it will be discussed during our initial consultation. You can also read more here.
