What Happened to You Doesn't Have to Define What's Ahead

Trauma lives in the body and the nervous system long after the event itself is over. It shows up in relationships, in reactivity, in a low-level sense of threat that follows you even when you're safe. Trauma-Focused CBT can help you process what happened — not just manage the symptoms — and start building a life that isn't organized around it.

What Trauma Can Look Like

◆

Flashbacks, Nightmares, Intrusive Memories

The past breaking into the present — unwanted memories, vivid dreams, or moments where you're suddenly back in something you thought was behind you.

◆

Hypervigilance

Always scanning for danger. Startling easily. Difficulty being in crowded spaces. The nervous system stuck on high alert, even when the actual threat is long gone.

◆

Emotional Numbness or Disconnection

Feeling detached from your own life. Going through the motions. A sense that you're watching yourself from the outside — the brain's way of protecting itself from what's too much to feel.

◆

Difficulty Trusting or Feeling Safe

Relationships feel risky. Closeness feels dangerous. When someone has broken your sense of safety, rebuilding trust — with others and with yourself — takes real work.

◆

Avoiding Reminders

Steering clear of people, places, smells, sounds, or situations that bring it back. Avoidance keeps the pain at bay short-term, but tends to keep the trauma active over time.

◆

Relationship Patterns That Feel Hard to Break

Attracting or staying in harmful dynamics. Difficulty with intimacy or conflict. Trauma often shapes the way we relate to people — sometimes in ways we can't fully see until someone helps us look.

The approach

How We Work With Trauma

Trauma therapy at Watchman Counseling is grounded in Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT) and trauma-informed clinical care. The difference matters: trauma-informed means every part of the work — the pacing, the relationship, what we focus on and when — is shaped by an understanding of how trauma affects the brain and nervous system.

TF-CBT helps you process the traumatic narrative at a pace that's safe for you. We build coping skills first. We don't dive into the deep end before you're ready, and you're never pushed to go faster than you can handle. Processing trauma isn't about re-experiencing it raw — it's about working through it in a way that changes how it's stored and how much power it still holds.

Figueroa, LCSW brings clinical experience with trauma across a range of settings: substance use recovery (where trauma and addiction are deeply intertwined), domestic violence, childhood adversity, and community mental health. That breadth means the approach is flexible — shaped by what actually happened to you, not a template.

Some people come in knowing their trauma history. Others come in knowing something is wrong but not fully understanding why. Both are starting points. We'll figure out the shape of it together.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Trauma-Focused CBT? +
Trauma-Focused CBT (TF-CBT) is an evidence-based treatment developed specifically for trauma — including PTSD, childhood trauma, abuse, and exposure to violence. It combines cognitive and behavioral techniques with trauma processing in a structured, phased approach. The phases typically include building coping skills, psychoeducation about trauma, gradual processing of the traumatic experience, and integration. It's one of the most researched trauma treatments available.
Is telehealth therapy safe for trauma work? +
Yes — telehealth trauma therapy is evidence-supported and works as effectively as in-person treatment for most people. For survivors of trauma, being in your own space during sessions can actually feel safer and more grounding than a clinical office. The key is a secure, private environment and a good therapeutic relationship — both of which are fully achievable via telehealth.
What if I'm not ready to talk about what happened? +
That's completely okay — and honestly, that's where most people start. Trauma therapy doesn't begin with you telling the story. We build the foundation first: safety, coping tools, trust in the process. The processing work comes when you're ready for it, and you set the pace. There's no pressure to go somewhere before you have what you need to get there safely.
