The approach

How Faith-Integrated Therapy Works Here

Faith-integrated therapy means your beliefs are welcome in the room — not just tolerated, but actively used as a resource. We can draw on scripture, prayer, spiritual community, or theological frameworks as part of the therapeutic work, when that's what you want. The integration is always your choice, not mine.

Hector L. Figueroa, LCSW is a Chaplain with the New York State Chaplains Task Force and brings both clinical training and lived faith experience to this work. That means he understands what it means to hold belief alongside struggle — not in a theoretical way, but in a real one.

Sessions are built on CBT and evidence-based approaches. Faith doesn't replace clinical skill — it works alongside it. If you're dealing with anxiety, depression, trauma, or addiction, we're still doing the evidence-based work. Faith gives that work an additional dimension of meaning and grounding that secular therapy often can't offer.

Sessions are faith-neutral by default. If you want to incorporate your faith, say so and we'll make it central. If you're still figuring out what you believe, that's welcome too. There's no pressure to perform belief, and no judgment either way.

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to be religious to benefit from this? +
No. Sessions are faith-neutral by default. Faith integration is an option, not a requirement. If you're not religious, or you're in a complicated relationship with religion, that's completely fine — the clinical work stands on its own. This page exists for clients who want the option to bring their faith in; it's not a requirement for anyone.
What faith tradition do you work with? +
Hector's background is Christian, and he's most fluent in a Christian framework — he can work directly with scripture, Christian theology, and church community dynamics. That said, he works respectfully with people from other faith traditions as well. The goal is always to honor your belief system, not impose a different one.
Can therapy help with church hurt or religious trauma? +
Yes — and this is an area that secular therapists sometimes don't have the language or context to address well. Religious trauma, spiritual abuse, or painful experiences with faith communities are real and deserve careful, informed clinical attention. We can work through what happened, process the grief and anger, and help you figure out what — if anything — your relationship with faith looks like going forward. That's entirely your call.
