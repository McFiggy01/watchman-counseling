What Men's Issues Can Look Like

◆

Constant Pressure to Perform

At work, at home, in relationships. The expectation never lets up, and there's no room to admit you're struggling under it.

◆

Anger or Irritability

Not explosive necessarily — just a low-grade edge. Shorter fuse. Frustration that shows up sideways.

◆

Emotional Shutdown

Knowing something is wrong but not being able to name it. Going numb instead of feeling it. Building walls without meaning to.

◆

Relationship Strain

Pulling away from your partner, your kids, your friends. Not knowing how to connect the way you want to.

◆

Loss of Purpose or Direction

What am I doing this for? A quiet sense that something is missing even when things look fine on the outside.

◆

Stress Without an Off Switch

Carrying work stress, financial pressure, family obligations — and no real way to decompress. It follows you everywhere.

The approach

How We Work With Men's Issues

Therapy for men at Watchman Counseling starts with one thing: no judgment. A lot of men come in having never talked to anyone about what's actually going on. That's not a problem — it's the starting point. We move at a pace that makes sense for you.

We work on the practical stuff: stress management, communication, boundary-setting, emotional identification. Not in a theoretical way — in a "what's happening in your life right now and what do we do about it" way. CBT gives us a structure for understanding how your thinking is driving your behavior and how to shift it.

Men's issues often intersect with identity, purpose, and relationships — especially during major life transitions like a career change, divorce, fatherhood, or loss. These aren't just feelings problems. They're moments that require a clear head and real tools.

Hector L. Figueroa, LCSW brings a direct, no-nonsense approach to the work. He understands the pressures men carry and won't waste your time. The goal isn't to change who you are — it's to help you operate better.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is therapy for men different from regular therapy? +
Not fundamentally — but the focus shifts. Men's issues therapy tends to be more action-oriented and goal-driven, which fits how a lot of men prefer to work. We're not going to spend months talking about your feelings without direction. We identify what's going on, we build a plan, and we work the plan. The emotional depth is there — it just comes out through problem-solving, not processing for its own sake.
What if I'm not sure therapy is right for me? +
That's exactly why the free 15-minute consult exists. You don't have to commit to anything. We talk about what's going on, I explain how I work, and you decide if it makes sense. A lot of men who were skeptical find that having a direct, structured therapist who doesn't turn everything into feelings work is actually useful.
Do you work with men going through divorce or separation? +
Yes. Divorce and relationship breakdown is one of the most common reasons men come in — and one of the highest-stress transitions a person can go through. We can work on managing the emotional fallout, navigating co-parenting, rebuilding identity, and figuring out what comes next.
